## c.	How to act in non-conversational/evaluative design sessions
### i. age appropriate language
#### Notes from research
- A second problem is that children in this age group are very literal. For instance, when
asked if they had been on a school field trip, the answer was "no", since they had been on a
class field trip (Holoday & Turner, 1989). 
- Borges: **Link (open PDF):** <https://www.epicentro.iss.it/okkioallasalute/documenti/letteratura_scientifica/Borghers_questionnaires%20children.pdf>
    * Ages 8–11: Children can answer well-designed questions consistently. They still have trouble with negations and still read words literally. Negatively phrased items should be avoided, which is the opposite of standard practice with adults. 
- Kendrick: **Link:** <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-minors/>
    * Tip 15, friendly and neutral: Do not sound like an authority figure. Say there are no right or wrong answers. Children under 5 went quiet when the facilitator kept a poker face, so use generic praise that does not steer them.
